PHI Access Monitoring

HIPAA, PHI, and public-documentation notice

HIPAA screens may display PHI, workforce identities, IP addresses, devices, routes, event metadata, before-and-after values, and other protected operational evidence. Screenshots in this public guide use redacted or demonstration information. Production access must be Provider-scoped, role-based, purpose-limited, auditable, and restricted to the minimum information reasonably necessary.

Compliance-supporting architecture

True Care System is designed to operationalize privacy, security, accountability, and minimum-necessary controls through Provider isolation, role-based permissions, PHI access logging, authentication monitoring, detailed audit history, support-session tracking, and security-event review. These capabilities support—but do not replace—the Provider's required policies, risk analysis, workforce training, business associate agreements, sanctions, incident response, and legal review.

Purpose

PHI Access Monitoring gives Privacy and Compliance personnel a focused view of workforce access to protected health information. It identifies who accessed PHI, which Individual was involved, the module and action, the stated purpose, the access decision, device evidence, and whether minimum-necessary expectations were met.

PHI Access History

The result table displays Date/Time, User, Role, Provider, Individual, Module, Action, Purpose, Decision, Minimum Necessary, IP Address, and Device. Provider scope and access controls are enforced by the backend, not only by the visual filter.

Privacy Rule and Minimum Necessary Oversight

The Privacy Rule limits uses and disclosures of PHI and gives individuals rights over their information. True Care System supports operational oversight by recording the reason for access and surfacing events that may require review. The Provider remains responsible for defining job duties, approved purposes, access policies, and documented exceptions.

Examples of Review Questions

  • Was the user assigned to the Individual or authorized workflow?
  • Was the access for Treatment, Payment, or Health Care Operations?
  • Did the user access more records than reasonably necessary?
  • Was the access performed from an expected device and location?
  • Was an export, print, or support session appropriately approved and logged?
