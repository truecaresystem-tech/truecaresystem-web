HIPAA, PHI, and public-documentation notice

HIPAA screens may display PHI, workforce identities, IP addresses, devices, routes, event metadata, before-and-after values, and other protected operational evidence. Screenshots in this public guide use redacted or demonstration information. Production access must be Provider-scoped, role-based, purpose-limited, auditable, and restricted to the minimum information reasonably necessary.

Compliance-supporting architecture

True Care System is designed to operationalize privacy, security, accountability, and minimum-necessary controls through Provider isolation, role-based permissions, PHI access logging, authentication monitoring, detailed audit history, support-session tracking, and security-event review. These capabilities support—but do not replace—the Provider's required policies, risk analysis, workforce training, business associate agreements, sanctions, incident response, and legal review.

Purpose

HIPAA Audit Logs provide a searchable record of PHI access, user activity, support impersonation, administrative actions, and system changes. The page helps authorized reviewers reconstruct who did what, when, to which record, under which Provider, and with what result.

Audit Results

The results table shows Date/Time, User, Actor, Target, Provider, Individual, Employee, Module, Action, Severity, and Status. The distinction between User, Actor, and Target is especially important during support impersonation or delegated administrative activity.

Compliance Use Cases

  • Investigate an unexpected update or deletion.
  • Confirm whether a user viewed an Individual's PHI.
  • Trace support access performed during an approved impersonation session.
  • Review failed or denied actions.
  • Prepare evidence for an internal audit, security investigation, complaint response, or breach-risk assessment.
